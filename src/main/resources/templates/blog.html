1. About Typo.css

p { color: red }

The goal of Typo.css is to normalize browser typography while building a layout best suited for comfortable on-screen reading.

Current state and the approach:

Typography is a tricky problem # Appendix 1 that requires careful design, yet that design is often overlooked by visual designers. Front-end engineers run into these issues more often but find them inconvenient to change, because rendering differs across browsers on multiple operating systems and any change requires a lot of regression testing time, which makes change even harder. The Reset styles commonly used in Yahoo, Eric Meyer, and Alice base.css do not consider typography well. What Typo.css aims to do is solve those typography problems.

Key points and difficulties in typography

In web typography, many HTML4 semantics are well covered. But visually, it is hard to achieve some effects with CSS alone, such as the emphasis mark (for example: emphasizing this). In HTML4 the proper-noun-underline tag (<u>) was deprecated, and in HTML5 it has been reintroduced. Typo.css also provides solutions matched to real-world situations. Two important things to watch for are:

  1. Semantics: whether the usage and style attached to each semantic tag are consistent with typographic conventions
  2. Presentation: whether font, size, and scaling behave as the typography expects across browsers

For these, the typography focus points of the Typo.css project have all been added to the appendix. See:

Appendix 1: Typo.css typography focus points

There are already projects making full use of Typo.css, but test platform coverage, especially on mobile, has not yet reached the major platforms. We hope capable contributors will join the testing effort or get involved in Typo.css development. To get started: contribute to Typo.css. If you have critiques, suggestions, or feedback, feel free to file issues directly on GitHub, or send me an email.

Example 1: Analects, "Xue Er", chapter one

Author: Confucius ( 28 September 551 BC – 11 April 479 BC )

Introduction to this chapter

"Xue Er" is the title of the first chapter of the Analects. Each chapter of the Analects is generally named after the first two or three characters of its opening passage. The "Xue Er" chapter contains 16 sections and covers many subjects. Its main themes are "I examine myself three times a day"; "be frugal and love people, employ them at the right time"; "in the use of ritual, harmony is most valued"; together with benevolence, filial piety, trustworthiness, and other moral categories.

Original text

The Master said: "Is it not a pleasure to learn and to put what one has learned into practice from time to time? Is it not a joy to have like-minded friends come from afar? Is it not the mark of a gentleman that one does not resent being unrecognized by others?"

Translation

Confucius said: "Is it not delightful to study and practice frequently what you have learned? Is it not a pleasure to have like-minded friends come from far away? When others do not understand me, I feel no resentment or anger — is that not the mark of a virtuous gentleman?"

Commentary

The famous Song-dynasty scholar Zhu Xi placed great value on this chapter, calling it "the gateway to the Way and the foundation of virtue." These three sentences are very familiar to most readers. The traditional reading goes: after learning, reviewing and practicing regularly — is that not a pleasure? — and so on. Each of the three sentences carries its own meaning, and the sentences are not strongly connected. But some have argued this reading does not match the original sense, pointing out that "learning" here does not mean studying, but rather a doctrine or position; "time" does not mean "frequently" but rather "the era" or "society"; and "practice" does not mean review but use, extended to mean adoption. Moreover, these three sentences are not isolated, but flow into one another. Their meaning then becomes: if my own teachings are adopted by society, that is a great joy; failing that, if many friends agree with my teachings and come to discuss them, I also feel happiness; and stepping back further, even if society does not adopt them and people do not understand me, I feel no resentment — is that not the mark of a gentleman? (See the article in the Qi-Lu Journal, 1986, issue 6.) This reading is internally consistent and has some merit, offered here for readers' reference when interpreting the chapter.

In addition, in interpreting the line "others do not know, yet one is not resentful," some commentators have noted that the line has no explicit object — others do not know what, exactly? Because Confucius spoke within a specific context at the time, he did not need to spell out what was known; his audience would have understood, but later generations are left with a puzzle. Some say this line follows directly from the one before: a friend comes from afar to seek instruction, I explain, he still does not understand, yet I feel no resentment. On that reading, "others do not know" means "others do not understand what I am explaining." This interpretation feels a bit strained.

In summary, this chapter holds up learning as a source of joy and being unbothered by others' lack of recognition, reflecting Confucius's view that one should never tire of learning, never grow weary of teaching, focus on self-cultivation, and hold oneself to a strict standard. These ideas appear in many places throughout the Analects and help illuminate the meaning of this opening chapter.

3. Appendix

1. Typo.css typography focus points
Category Semantic Tag Notes
Basic tags Headings h1 ~ h6 Sizes are not forced globally; inside .typo, headings should sit tight against their content and use proportional sizes
Superscript and subscript sup/sub Stays consistent with the everyday rendering of programs like Microsoft Office Word
Quote blockquote Display and nested styles
Abbreviation abbr Underlined throughout; cursor changes to a help pointer on hover
Divider hr Correct padding and margin on display
List ul/ol/dl No list-style globally; aligned correctly inside .typo
Definition list dl Global padding and margin set to 0; aligned correctly inside .typo
Form options input[type=radio[, checkbox]] Vertically centered when laid out alongside other form elements
Italic i Only one italic style is set, so em and cite render upright
Emphasis em Renders upright globally; inside .typo it matches b and strong as bold
Stronger emphasis strong/b Renders bold
Highlight mark Looks like a highlighter pen
Small print small Keeps the font at 80% of the correct size, with a light gray color
Table table No lines globally; inside a table the outer border is shown and the header has a light gray background
Code pre/code Uses a courier-family font, kept visually consistent with the serif body
Special marks Emphasis dots dot beneath the text Can be progressively enhanced in browsers that support :after and :before
Proper-name underline Lin Jianfeng Proper-name underline, with an underline, using the u tag or the .typo-u class
Em dash —— Render as a single stroke, not two
Yuan sign ¥ Use the two-parallel-line symbol, or the HTML entity &yen;
Strike-through deleted Normalize rendering across browsers; mixed-script lines display correctly
Utility classes Proper-name underline .typo-u Because u was deprecated in HTML4, .typo-u is recommended for backward compatibility
Emphasis dots .typo-em Uses :after and :before to draw emphasis dots
Clear floats .clearfix API kept consistent with common CSS Reset stylesheets
Watch out for (1) Line height and leading when mixing scripts
(2) Rendering of superscript/subscript in IE
(3) Whether the whitespace between blocks and paragraphs follows the design guidelines
(4) The extra space issue around input elements
(5) Default text color; #333 currently renders well across browsers
