1. About Typo.css

p { color: red }
p { color: red }

The goal of Typo.css is to unify typography across browsers while building a web layout best suited for long-form reading.

Current state and what to do:

Typography is a tricky problem # Appendix 1. It needs careful design, yet visual designers often overlook it. Front-end engineers see these issues more often but find them hard to change, because different browsers across different operating systems render text differently and any change requires extensive regression testing. The Reset rules in commonly used base stylesheets such as Yahoo, Eric Meyer, and Alice base.css do not pay enough attention to non-Latin text layout. Typo.css sets out to solve exactly that problem.

Key points and difficulties of typography

In typography for long-form text, HTML4 covers many semantic needs, but the visual side is hard to achieve with CSS alone. Examples include emphasis marks (for example: here we emphasize a word). In HTML4, the proper-noun tag (<u>) was deprecated, while in HTML5 it has been brought back. Typo.css provides matching solutions based on real-world needs. There are two important things to watch out for:

  1. Semantics: whether the intended usage and styling align with the conventions of the text you are setting.
  2. Presentation: whether the font, size, and scaling render in different browsers the way the layout expects.

Example 1: An opening chapter from a classic text

Author: Confucius ( September 28, 551 BC – April 11, 479 BC )

Introduction to this chapter

This is the opening chapter of a well-known classical collection. Chapters in the collection are typically named after the first few characters of their opening sentence. The chapter contains sixteen short passages covering a wide range of topics. The central themes include daily self-reflection, mindful use of resources and care for others, the value of harmony in ritual, and moral concepts such as benevolence, filial piety, and trustworthiness.

Original passage

The teacher said: "Is it not a pleasure to study and to put what you learn into practice from time to time? Is it not a joy when like-minded friends come from afar? Is it not the mark of a noble person to remain unbothered when others do not recognize you?"

Translation

The teacher said: "Reviewing and practicing what you have learned — is that not a happy thing? When kindred spirits travel from afar to see you — is that not a delight? When people fail to understand you and you do not resent them — is that not the conduct of a virtuous person?"

Commentary

The Song-dynasty scholar Zhu Xi rated this chapter very highly, calling it "the gateway to the way and the foundation of virtue." The three sentences are familiar to almost everyone. The traditional reading is: studying and then revisiting what you have learned is a happy thing, and so on — three lines, three meanings, with little connection between them. Other readers argue this misses the original sense and suggest that "study" here refers not to study itself but to one's teachings or beliefs; "time" refers not to "often" but to one's era or society; and "practice" means not review but adoption. On that reading the three sentences are connected: if society adopts one's teachings, that is wonderful; if it does not, but friends who share one's views travel from afar to discuss them, that is still a delight; and even if society does not adopt them and people do not understand, remaining unresentful is itself the conduct of a virtuous person. (See an article in the sixth 1986 issue of a regional academic journal.) This reading is internally consistent and has some merit; it is offered as a reference for readers thinking through this chapter.

Separately, on the phrase "people do not understand and yet one is not resentful," some readers note that the phrase has no explicit object — not understand what? Because the original speaker had a specific context in mind, he did not need to spell it out; his listeners understood, but later readers were left to guess. One suggestion is that the phrase continues from the previous one: a friend comes from afar to ask for guidance, I explain it and he still does not grasp it, yet I am not resentful. On that reading, "people do not understand" simply means "people do not understand what I am explaining." That reading feels a little forced.

In sum, this chapter presents learning as a source of joy and remaining unresentful when others do not understand. It reflects the speaker's love of learning, tireless teaching, attention to personal cultivation, and high standards for himself. These ideas recur throughout the rest of the collection and help readers understand this opening chapter more deeply.

3. Appendix

1. Typo.css typography focus points
Type Semantics Tag What to watch out for
Base tags Headings h1 – h6 No forced global size; in .typo, headings should sit close to their content and have matching size settings
Superscript / subscript sup/sub Keep consistent with typical Word-processor typography
Quotation blockquote Display and nesting style
Abbreviation abbr Whether it is underlined, and whether the cursor turns into a help icon on hover
Horizontal rule hr Correct padding and margin on display
Lists ul/ol/dl No global list-style; correct alignment inside .typo
Definition list dl Global padding and margin are 0; correct alignment inside .typo
Inputs input[type=radio[, checkbox]] Whether they align vertically with other form elements
Italic i Use a single italic style; let em and cite display upright
Emphasis em Upright globally; inside .typo, render bold to match b and strong
Strong strong/b Rendered bold
Highlight mark Like a highlighter pen
Small print small Hold at 80% of the surrounding font size with a light-grey color
Table table No global borders; inside table show an outer border and use a light-grey header background
Code pre/code Use a courier-family font that pairs visually with serif body text
Special marks Emphasis dots Dot under each character Browsers that support :after and :before can use a progressive-enhancement implementation
Proper-noun underline Lin Jianfeng Proper-noun marker with an underline; use u or the .typo-u class
Em dash —— Keep as a single stroke, not two
Yuan / Yen sign ¥ Use the two-bar symbol, or the HTML entity &yen;
Strikethrough deleted Render consistently across browsers; works correctly in mixed Latin / CJK text
Enhancement classes Proper-noun marker .typo-u Since u was deprecated in HTML4, use .typo-u for backward compatibility
Emphasis marker .typo-em Implements emphasis dots via :after and :before
Clear floats .clearfix Matches the API used by typical CSS resets
Things to watch (1) Line height / leading in mixed Latin / CJK text
(2) How superscript and subscript render in IE
(3) Whether block / paragraph spacing matches the intended design
(4) Extra whitespace around input elements
(5) Default text color — #333 currently renders well across browsers
